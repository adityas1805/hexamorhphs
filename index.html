The gap

Instruments confirm hazards.
People see them first.

01

Sensors are sparse

Buoys and tide gauges cover fixed points. A rip current at an unmonitored beach, a sudden swell surge past a fishing jetty — none of it is instrumented.

02

Ground-truth gets lost

Fishermen, coastal residents and tourists witness hazards constantly, but there's no single channel for that observation to reach INCOIS before it's outdated.

03

Public chatter is unread

Panic, false alarms and real early warnings all surface on social platforms and local news in parallel — unread by disaster managers until it's already trending.

The system

Three feeds. One chart.

FEED 01

Citizen reporting

Geo-tagged photo/video hazard reports from a lightweight app — works offline, syncs when signal returns, queued for coastal dead zones.

  • Hazard type, severity, geotag, timestamp
  • Offline-first capture & sync queue
  • Community verification / trust score
FEED 02

Signal analytics

NLP pipeline scans public news and social chatter (Google News + Reddit) for hazard-relevant keywords, scoring severity, region and recency.

  • Multilingual keyword & entity extraction
  • Severity classification (major / minor)
  • Geo-clustering by coastal region
FEED 03

Ops dashboard

INCOIS analysts see a unified hazard map: verified reports, unverified sightings and news/social hotspots overlaid, ranked by urgency.

  • Live hazard map with confidence layer
  • Report triage & verification queue
  • Alert dispatch to affected zones

Feed 03, in miniature

Alerts & preparedness

If a major hazard is active near you

  • Move away from the shoreline and low-lying coastal roads immediately
  • Do not go out to fish or swim until an official all-clear
  • Follow INCOIS / district disaster-management advisories, not just social media
  • Keep a charged phone, ID, and essential documents ready if evacuation is advised
  • Check on neighbours who may not have received the alert

Get notified for your region

Sign up with an email or phone number and a region — you'll be first to know when a major hazard is detected there.
